    No. 01

    Axe Dragon

    Securis caput

    I first heard sign of this dragon from camp in the early morning; a loud whacking noise echoed through the canyon from a small grove of trees nearby. I climbed a rock to spy on the creature. The noise was its head, shaped exactly like a sharpened axe, widely swinging and connecting with the trunk of a mature Joshua tree. I watched long enough for it to fell the tree and begin chewing at the base. It seems to have found grubs burrowed inside, but it eats much of the tree in the process. It may be adapted to digest the tree wood and grub meat together.

    It is perceptive and territorial. I was hastily chased away after it noticed me nearby. Fortunately it did not appear to have any means of breath attack, though it did fling some rocks toward me as I fled.

    I caught sight of a dragon of this same species with an egg. It used the end of its tail to tap on the egg with a distinct pattern, repeating it many times. I can only guess about why it does this.

    Habitat
    Wooded areas
    Eats
    Grubs, from rotting logs

    No. 02

    Mushroom Dragon

    Draco fungorum

    In a rather unpleasant first meeting, I happened upon this dragon while traveling back to camp in the evening. It stood perfectly still. I had assumed it to be some oversized fungi influenced by the ecology of the dragon wilds. Reaching out to touch it was my mistake. It reacted with a violent cloud of pungent dust. The coughing lasted a few days, but I eventually recovered.

    I pay careful attention to these creatures now. They are rarely hostile, and move in short, deliberate strides.

    What they eat remains a mystery to me. Given their form, perhaps their nutrition comes from what they stand on.

    Habitat
    Multiple
    Eats
    Undetermined

    No. 03

    Sand Swimmer

    Harenae natatorus

    My only sight of this species so far was while I was hiking, looking down into a canyon at a windswept sandy dune that was nestled up against the canyon wall. Just before I turned to leave, I noticed some movement out of the corner of my eye. I saw two large eyes moving about the top of the sand. Whatever was underneath seemed to move around in it like it was wading in water. After a moment, its head sank, disappearing from view.

    I waited and waited, watching to see if the dragon would reveal itself. As it got dark, our camp ranger urged me to return. Just before I was dragged away, I threw a rock over to the dune to see if I could get the dragon to react. It leapt from the sand, caught the rock in its mouth, and dove under again. It had been ready that entire time. If only my colleagues were similarly patient.

    Habitat
    Sand
    Eats
    Unknown

    No. 04

    Lilypad Dragon

    Draco nymphaea

    My first encounter with this species was early in the morning, the sun barely rising from behind the horizon. As I collected samples of water and soil from a nearby lake, giant lily pads began to sway and shift, reacting to the sunlight. I watched motionless as several heads breached the water, the lily pads being dragged with their bodies as they swam around the lake.

    They seem to have some symbiotic relationship with the plant life growing on them, with a rare few sporting large flowers as well. Could it be possible that they are born with them?

    It's not clear what their diet is. Whatever they eat seems to be consumed as they are underwater. I will need to find a way to get a closer look, but safely. Attempts to approach the dragons caused them to shoot highly pressurized beams of water toward me. I was lucky to avoid their attack, but they did manage to ruin the original notebook detailing their behavior. A frustrating setback.

    Habitat
    Ponds/Water - Near lily pads
    Eats
    Undetermined
