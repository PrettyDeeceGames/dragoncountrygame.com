    PH · introI have kept these notes since I first came to Dragon Country. Each dragon I set down as I found it: where it lives, what it eats, and how it changes. Where I could not learn a thing, I have said so.

    listen for knocking

    No. 01

    Axe Dragon

    PHDraco securis
    PH · entry

    I found this dragon first by its sound, a steady knocking in the timber like a woodsman at work. When I came near I saw it was no man but a dragon, steel-blue and as long as a cart, striking a rotten log with the hammer at the end of its tail. The wood broke apart and it ate the white grubs living inside, and when there were no more it went on to the next log.

    The head is shaped like the blade of an axe, and it uses it on standing trees in the same manner.

    I once watched a mother at her nest tap her egg with that same tail, very gently and in a kind of rhythm. I could not learn why. I have written the rhythm down and mean to try it myself.

    No. 02

    Mushroom Dragon

    PHDraco fungorum
    PH · entry

    This one I have seen only at the ranch, inside the pen, and never in the wild. It carries a broad cap upon its head, ribbed beneath like a mushroom, and when it stands still in the shade one could take it for one.

    Where it comes from and what it becomes, I could not yet learn, though I watched it for many days. I will write more when I know more.

    Sand Swimmer

    PH · reveal callworking name
    PH · entry

    A traveller told me of a dragon that lives under the sand and lets only its eyes show above it, two great round eyes that do not blink. It waits there without moving until something steps too near, and then it bites.

    He made this drawing for me from memory. I have not seen it myself, and I set it down here so that others may take care in the sand.

    Lilypad Dragon

    PH · reveal callworking name
    PH · entry

    At the pond in the early morning I saw what I took to be a great clump of lily pads drifting against the wind. When I came nearer, a head rose from among the leaves and looked at me with half-closed eyes, as though I had woken it.

    It did not seem troubled by me. Before I could come closer it sank back under the leaves, and I saw no more of it. These drawings I made that same evening, from memory.
